Economic Impact Analysis

Data-driven economic modeling to understand how policies, projects, and investments affect your local economy.

Overview

Economic impact analysis quantifies how a proposed project, policy, or event will affect a local or regional economy. Whether you're considering a new infrastructure investment, evaluating a major employer's expansion, or assessing the economic footprint of an annual event, our analyses provide the rigorous, defensible numbers you need to make informed decisions and communicate with stakeholders.

We measure direct effects—the immediate spending and employment associated with an activity—as well as indirect and induced effects that ripple through supply chains and household spending. The result is a comprehensive picture of jobs supported, income generated, and tax revenues produced.

Our Approach

Defining the Study Area and Baseline

Every impact analysis begins with clearly defining the geographic scope and establishing baseline economic conditions. We work with you to identify the appropriate boundaries—whether a single municipality, a county, or a multi-jurisdictional region—and gather data on current employment, output, and industry composition.

Input-Output Modeling

We employ industry-standard input-output models to trace how spending flows through interconnected sectors of the economy. These models capture the relationships between industries, allowing us to calculate how an initial expenditure generates additional economic activity as businesses purchase inputs from suppliers and workers spend their wages locally.

Estimating Multiplier Effects

Our analyses distinguish between Type I multipliers (direct plus indirect effects) and Type II multipliers (which also include induced household spending effects). We select the appropriate multiplier framework based on the nature of your project and clearly document all assumptions so results can withstand scrutiny.

Fiscal Impact Assessment

Beyond jobs and economic output, we estimate effects on local government revenues—including sales taxes, property taxes, and income taxes where applicable—as well as any increased demand for public services. This fiscal dimension helps you understand whether a project will be a net positive for the public balance sheet.
